# The independent recount of the 5.5 replication, and the four disclosures

Every figure in the replication's report was re-derived before it was cited: an
AI agent separate from the one that ran the study, in a fresh context, wrote
fresh code from the registration (the locked text and Amendments A1 and A2) and
the frozen rules it cites, and ran it on the committed run data. It made no
model calls and read no credentials. "Independent" here means separate code and
context, not a separate person or team. This note says how it worked, what it
found, and what it did not verify.

**Verdict: confirmed.** All 5,840 scored P/S rows agree on status, adjudicated
verdict, route and the key-matched-wrong-family flag. 523 of 526 compared
figures agree at full precision; the other 3 are the recount's first reading of
two plain-review figures under a rule the registration leaves ambiguous, and its
second reading matches the report exactly. The seeded bootstrap intervals match
to the last digit. None of the divergences changes a headline, an interval, a
verb or a gate.

## Protocol

- **Blind phase.** The recount wrote its own code from the registration and the
  frozen rules. Its outputs (every figure, and one row per scored question)
  were sealed by SHA-256 before it opened the report or any of the study's
  analysis code. After the seal, its code was edited once, only to add three
  fields to the row dump; the figures file regenerated byte-identical.
- **Framed phase.** Only then did it compare: 526 figure checks of the report
  against its own figures, and a row-by-row diff against the study's rows,
  which it produced by running the study's scorer read-only. The false-alarm
  multi-file / single-function split and its bound states were added in this
  phase (the blind pass missed the split), computed from the recount's own rows
  with the original study's frozen bound check.
- **Independence limit, stated plainly.** For P and S, the recount imported the
  original study's frozen scoring modules (parser, scorer, presence layer, and
  the adjudication-batch builder and scrub) as the rule of record, the same
  modules the study imports. Agreement on mechanically scored rows is therefore
  partly by construction. Re-derived independently:
  - record adaptation and the status partition, from the raw response fields;
  - cost, from usage × the pinned prices;
  - the lane verdicts, re-parsed from the readers' raw text;
  - the merge;
  - V2 and V3;
  - every aggregate, the bootstrap and the gates;
  - the plain scorer (its own patterns, cross-checked against the calibration
    study's scorer with 0 mismatches);
  - false alarms, the bridge, the lane, the clean-item pair check and spend.

## Divergences, classified

**D1 (definition; registration ambiguity, no code defect): the plain strict
rate at Sonnet 5.5.** The registration says "rate = hits / parseable". The
recount read it as pooled over included items, 56 / 97 = 0.5773; the report
uses the mean of per-item rates, 0.5707. The recount's item-mean variant equals
the report exactly. Only Sonnet 5.5 differs, because only there do items fall
under the inclusion floor (33 of 36 included) and carry unequal parseable
counts. The zero-spend reproduction gate cannot pin the reading: on the
calibration records Haiku 4.5 gives 0.57900 by item mean and 0.57921 pooled,
and both round to the registered 0.579. The catch-rate table, which the
registration does define per item averaged over repetitions, is consistent with
the report's reading.

**D2 (definition and judgment; an unregistered figure on a mixed basis): plain
true catches and dollars per catch at Sonnet 5.5.** The report prints 57 plain
true catches and $0.01483 per catch. The 57 counts hits over all 36 defective
items, including the 3 items the strict rate excludes under the inclusion
floor; on included items only it is 56 and $0.01509 per catch. The
registration defines dollars per true catch for P and S target-family catches
only; a plain dollars-per-catch figure is not registered. (The public summary
does not print it.)

**D3 (definition; scope of the clean-item pair check; no printed figure
affected).** The check ran on the 17 main-tier clean-item false-alarm pairs.
Eight clean-item pairs that occur only in the Haiku 4.5 bridge were never
checked (`clean-mf-05` × boundary, comparison, units; `clean-sf-09` ×
boundary; `clean-sf-10` × comparison, exception, sign, units). The bridge read
does not include false alarms and none is printed, so nothing moves; if bridge
false alarms are ever printed, they stand at the not-real default, unchecked.

**D4 (reporting gap).** The registration asks for the V2/V3 co-reads beside the
false alarms; the report printed them only beside the S/P ratio and the catch
rates. The recount's V2/V3 false-alarm reads at Haiku 5.5, pooled over mf and
sf: V3 lowers S off-family false alarms from 67 to 61 of 708 and S clean-item
any-flag from 32/60 to 28/60; V2 raises P key-matched-wrong-family flags from 3
to 10 of 708. The recount judged that no false-alarm state is likely to flip at
Haiku 5.5, because the S − P gaps are large, but it did not run the split bound
check under V2 or V3. Also not printed in the report: the arm-guess accuracy
against chance. 5 of 42 guesses were correct overall, 5 of the 9 committed
guesses, with 33 declined; chance over the two arms is 1/2, so there is no
evidence the lane could infer the arm.

**A study-side rule the registration does not state (moot here).** The study's
scorer marks a sampled row UNRESOLVED when the second reader disagrees and no
third-reader verdict exists. The registration is silent on this case; it
affected 0 rows.

## What the recount did not verify

- Payload and request bytes against the frozen assembly (the registration's
  "byte-for-byte"); the recount did not rebuild the payloads.
- The preflight accept path, the projection-gate arithmetic, and the
  reservation and halt logic; it checked settled totals only.
- How the arm-guess request was carried over the raw Messages API (prompt
  contents not inspected).
- The prompt composition of the adjudication lane and the pair check (batch
  contents only).
- The frozen scorer's own correctness: certified by the original study's
  recount (7,340 rows), and relied on here.
- The bridge ratios and their intervals, which the recount computed but the
  report does not print (at the Opus 5 bridge, 259 of 10,000 draws were
  undefined, so no interval would print).

## The four disclosures

Raised when the study was closed, from the recount. None changes a printed
headline.

1. **Plain strict rate aggregation (registration ambiguity).** The report reads
   "rate = hits / parseable" as the mean of per-item rates (Sonnet 5.5: 0.571);
   pooled it is 56/97 = 0.577. The zero-spend reproduction gate cannot
   discriminate (0.57900 vs 0.57921, both 0.579). Plain levels are therefore
   cited to two decimals.
2. **Plain dollars per catch is unregistered.** The registration defines
   dollars per catch for P and S target catches only. The report's plain column
   counts hits on all items (Sonnet 5.5: 57 catches, $0.01483 per catch); on
   the inclusion-floor items only it is 56 and $0.01509. It is descriptive
   context, not an estimand, and the public summary omits it.
3. **Scope of the clean-item pair check.** "17 of 17" is the main-tier queue.
   The 8 clean-item false-alarm pairs that occur only in the Haiku 4.5 bridge
   were not checked; no bridge false alarms are printed, so no figure moves.
4. **The false-alarm co-reads were not printed in the report.** From the
   recount, at Haiku 5.5: V3 lowers S off-family false alarms 67 → 61 and S
   clean-item any-flag 32/60 → 28/60; V2 raises P key-matched-wrong-family
   3 → 10. The Haiku 5.5 serialized false-alarm excess has not been
   bound-checked under V2 or V3: neither the study nor the recount re-ran the
   bound check under the co-reads. The recount judges a change of stamp
   unlikely, because the S − P gaps are large, but no computation backs that.
